Artist Statement

My work begins with light — the way it settles across a room in late afternoon, or falls unevenly on a table left half-cleared. I paint to slow that moment down.

I work primarily in oil and acrylic, building up thin, deliberate layers until a surface starts to hold its own kind of weather. Much of what I make sits between representation and memory: a coastline I return to every summer, a kitchen that no longer exists, a field seen once from a moving car. I am less interested in painting a place exactly as it was than in painting how it felt to be there.

Color, for me, is the primary language. I tend toward warm, earthen grounds — umbers, ochres, and deep greens — punctuated by a single unexpected note that pulls the whole canvas into focus. Texture matters just as much: I want a viewer standing close enough to see the brushwork to have as rich an experience as one standing across the room taking in the whole composition.

In recent years, sculpture has entered the studio practice as a natural extension of these same questions — how weight, surface, and light behave differently in three dimensions. Working between painting and sculpture keeps both practices honest; each discipline asks something the other cannot.

Ultimately, I make work for people who are willing to stand still for a minute. That stillness is, I think, the whole point.

— Elsie Whitfield

Professional Correspondence

Cover Letter

Elsie Whitfield
Studio 4B, Riverside Arts Building
New Orleans, LA

August 17, 2026

To the Selection Committee,

I am writing to submit my work for your consideration. Over the past decade, my studio practice has centered on oil painting and, more recently, sculpture — both grounded in an ongoing interest in light, domestic memory, and the textures of place. This body of work represents the most complete articulation of those ideas to date, and I believe it aligns closely with the goals of this opportunity.

My paintings have been shown in a number of regional galleries and juried exhibitions, and I have completed two short-term studio residencies focused on landscape and still life. Alongside the studio work, I have taught painting fundamentals to adult students, an experience that has sharpened both my technical vocabulary and my ability to talk clearly about process and intent.

I am especially drawn to this program's emphasis on sustained studio time and cross-disciplinary exchange. My current work is beginning to move between two and three dimensions, and I would welcome the chance to develop that direction further within a community of engaged practitioners.

Thank you for your time and consideration. I have included images of recent work, an artist statement, and a current CV for your review, and I would be glad to provide any additional materials upon request.

Sincerely,
Elsie Whitfield
